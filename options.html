Option 1

Build new — pre-engineered metal building

New 32,000 sf church real bids

$4.7M – $6.8M
All-in, land included. JOMA (Houston GC) $4,697,000 ($147/sf) · target Amarillo GC $4.98M–$5.85M ($155–183/sf) · Caprock (local, full GMP) $6,807,000 ($215/sf).

A pre-engineered metal building (PEMB) with rigid frames. The frames clear-span the width, so the worship room is column-free by design — the exact problem we fight in the theater simply does not exist here.

How it wins

  • Wide-open worship room, no posts, for $0 extra
  • Brand new, exactly to our plan, zero retrofit risk
  • Lowest hard cost per the real bids
  • Modern systems, low maintenance from day one

How it loses

  • Lower bid (JOMA) excludes some scope (2nd floor, landscaping); change orders can lift it
  • Smaller building (32,000 sf vs 83,000)
  • Full design + permit + build timeline before we worship in it
  • No existing parking/site improvements included
To confirm: that the bids include land/site as quoted. Final GC selection (bids range $147–215/sf). Whether 32,000 sf meets long-term growth.
Option 2

Buy & convert the theater

Former Amarillo Star — 83,422 sf real LOI

$5.3M – $10.3M all-in
Buy $3.8M (LOI; listed $5.5M) + sanctuary fit-out $1.5M–$3.5M estimate + column option below. Gets the sanctuary done; the other ~50,000 sf is additional/phased.

Convert the four center auditoriums into one sanctuary. The building already includes the structure, sprinklers, big HVAC, an elevator, and a huge parking lot. The catch is the 17 steel columns in the sanctuary — handling them is the swing:

Column approachResultAdded cost
A · aisles on the column linesPosts stand in aisles, still in the room~$0 est
B · remove center + front postsClear view to the stage; posts remain at back/edges$300K–$700K est
C · remove every post (clear span)Fully open, column-free room$1.2M–$3M est

How it wins

  • 83,422 sf and a big parking lot — 2x+ the new build
  • ~45,000 sf beyond the sanctuary for kids, offices, café, growth, even income
  • Land is included in the buy
  • Can phase: worship in the sanctuary while the rest is finished
  • Far more building for the money: 83,000 sf + an existing parking lot vs 32,000 sf new (the extra ~50,000 sf is unfinished/phased)

How it loses

  • The column problem costs $0.3M–$3M to truly solve, plus engineering
  • Retrofit risk: inspection flagged a major foundation crack + electrical item
  • Old building — ongoing maintenance, aging systems
  • More square footage than we may want to heat/cool/maintain
  • Clear-span (C) is a real structural unknown until an engineer prices it
To confirm: structural engineer's price + sign-off on column removal and the foundation crack; a real GC bid on the fit-out; final negotiated purchase price.
Seating study

A corner stage: fewer posts, better sightlines

You don't have to combine four rooms or remove 17 posts to seat the church. The steel columns sit on the walls between rooms, so each big auditorium is already a clear, open room. Here is how a corner (diagonal) stage plays out:

What you're seeing: the two center rooms (RPX + IMAX) opened into one, with the stage in a corner and the seats fanned toward it. About 1,180 seats, and the only 2 interior posts both land in the aisles. A corner stage trades a few raw seats (a straight front-wall layout fits ~1,310 in the same room) for better sightlines and an intimate, everyone-facing-in feel. Want zero posts at all? A single auditorium with a corner stage seats roughly 800 with nothing to work around. Seat counts are planning estimates; a licensed structural engineer confirms the structure before anything is removed.

Option 3

Master-lease the theater (then option to buy)

Lease first, buy later pro forma

~$20,856 / mo + reno
~$3/sf gross master lease with option to buy at $3.8M; rent abatement negotiable during construction. From the building committee pro forma.

Same building and same conversion as Option 2, but we lease it (with an option to buy) instead of buying up front. The renovation still happens; ownership comes later at the option price.

How it wins

  • Lowest cash up front — fastest way to get in the door
  • Walkable: if reno overruns or growth stalls, we can leave without a multi-million deficiency
  • Rent abatement during construction can keep year-one cash positive
  • Try the building before fully committing

How it loses

  • Rent paid during the lease isn't building equity
  • The buyout refi is the hardest exit of the three (per the pro forma)
  • Improving a building we don't yet own
  • Same column + retrofit issues as Option 2
To confirm: will the seller master-lease, and at what rent/abatement? (A 15-minute call to the listing agent collapses this question.)
